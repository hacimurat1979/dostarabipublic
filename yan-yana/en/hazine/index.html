What did not fit on the pages

This section is not a reading summary. It is an attempt to read Dost's words side by side with the findings of physics that do not fit reason; it is our own reading frame. We do not say “the same” or “confirmed”: we only ask, and we say where they part ways. The text is our own rendering of a Turkish original.

This page is not a commentary but a record. While reading the Futuhat we collected the hardest places that did not fit our reason, those that did not make it into the pages on the forms, in the eight densest sections. Each line carries Dost's sentence (or a short summary of it) and the form label we gave that sentence. We have not read any of them side by side with physics, and we say nothing about any of them.

The meaning of the labels is on the introduction page. The section number Vol. 12, Part 147 = volume 12, section 147; if you click the number you go to the page of that section.

Vol. 12, Part 147 — Point and Circumference: The Circle Diagram Dost Drew Himself

  • Of the three letters in the verse "Be", two are visible and one is hidden; al-Dahr (Time) brings out the precedence, delay and order in speech; Dost relays the Prophet's words, "Do not curse Time, for Time is Allah." (p. 332-334) (form: Time, Relation)
«Even if these causes are above you in terms of the rank that appears, the aim is you, the existent; those causes exist because of you and so that you may appear.» (Vol. 12, Part 147, p. 286-287)

Form: Cause, Time.

«al-Haqq conceals Himself in the form of the perfect human, that is, in the thingness of his fixity (ayn-ı sabite).» (Vol. 12, Part 147)

Form: Both-and, Inside-outside, Viewpoint.

«The perfect human has gathered all of this in himself by his very essence. This quality is not found in anyone else. Before al-Haqq he is a like, an opposite and a contrary.» (Vol. 12, Part 147, p. 296-297)

Form: Both-and, Relation.

«He is the perfect human and the deputy of al-Haqq. al-Haqq is his tongue and his powers.» (Vol. 12, Part 147, p. 298)

Form: Indistinguishability, Inside-outside.

«The world too is the barzakh between light and darkness. By its essence it can no more be described by darkness than by light.» (Vol. 12, Part 147)

Form: Both-and, Bewilderment.

«The point is al-Haqq; the void outside the circumference is non-existence, or darkness. Between the point and the void outside the circumference lies the possible... We mentioned the point because the point is the origin of the circumference of the circle. Just as the circle appears through the point, the possible appears through al-Haqq.» (Vol. 12, Part 147, p. 313-314)

Form: Relation, Wholeness.

«The point from which the line goes out toward the circumference is the beginning of the line. So it is the first and the last... The lines that go out, and the possibles, begin from Allah and return to Him. 'All things return to Him.'» (Vol. 12, Part 147, p. 314)

Form: Both-and, Time.

«For the whole of the world other than you consists of horizons, and they are your circumference.» (Vol. 12, Part 147, p. 311)

Form: Inside-outside, Relation.

«Existence is one-many... Just as we too exist by dependence (tâbi). Had it not been for al-Haqq, we would not be.» (Vol. 12, Part 147, p. 312-313)

Form: Both-and, Wholeness, Relation.

  • Animals, plants and inanimate things are in truth speaking beings (they perceive and speak); the people of unveiling and faith witness this. What shows life is called living; what keeps its life hidden is called plant or inert. (p. 264-266) (form: Viewpoint, Both-and)
  • The possibles are heard and seen by a hearing and seeing of fixity (subûtî) that has no outer existence; when al-Haqq's command "Be" is made audible to them, they rush to the command. (p. 265) (form: Both-and, Time)

Vol. 9, Part 124 — 'Searching Is Left to You': The Longest Section of Volume IX and Its Silent Closing

  • It is discussed that time and space have no outer existence and are of an imagined (mevhum) nature; the difference in motion and rest between the sphere and the element. (form: Time, Reason)
  • Chapter 255: Mahk means your becoming annihilated (fani) in Him, while mahku'l-mahk means your being fixed (sabit) in Him (p. 400); mahk is to appear with Him in becoming by way of caliphate and deputyship; the caliphate is tied to learning the meanings of the letters at the beginning of the surahs (Alif, Lam, Mim) (p. 401-402). At any one time only one person (the Ghawth, the Qutb) remains alone with al-Haqq (p. 402). (form: Both-and, Indistinguishability, Bewilderment)
  • Chapters 260–261: nearness (qurb) is the state of the two ends of a circle cut by a line, or of being closer (p. 411); But in truth there is no possibility of distance from Allah. Distance is a relative state (p. 415); Nearness is gathering, and distance is dispersal and separation; there is nothing in the world farther from Allah (p. 418). (form: Both-and, Viewpoint)
  • Chapter 264: All thoughts are divine addresses, not self-disclosures. For this reason Allah has constructed them as forms that come about in the Amā — which is the breath of the All-Merciful (p. 430). (form: Reason, Cause)
  • Chapter 199: The saying of Sahl b. Abdullah, "Lordship has a secret which, if it appeared, would make lordship void," is explained (p. 195); the secret is at three ranks: the secret of knowledge, the secret of state, the secret of reality (p. 193). (form: Knowledge, Bewilderment)
  • Chapter 202: the etiquette of reality is to see al-Haqq in things as the very self of things — not as them (p. 201). (form: Both-and, Indistinguishability, Viewpoint)
  • Chapter 206: In the year 593, in Fez, at the afternoon prayer, he saw the light behind him more clearly than the light in front of him and could no longer tell the directions apart: Rather I was as if a sphere (p. 213-214). The lights are counted as front, right, left, back, above and below according to their sources. (form: Containment, Bewilderment, Inside-outside)
  • Chapter 207: So the cause ('illa) is to take a reason to exist, while Allah is the reason itself (p. 223); Any reason that does not turn you back to Allah, warn you about Him or awaken a consciousness of Him is not a cause ('illa) (p. 224). (form: Cause, Reason)
  • Chapter 209: we said that witnessing (muşâhede) is the seeing of the witness. That witness is what is in the heart from al-Haqq (p. 237); Bilqis's throne, "as if it is it," and the Companions' saying of Gabriel in the form of Dihya, "it is Dihya," are compared. The chapter ends with You can never see al-Haqq and creation together (p. 238). (form: Viewpoint, Both-and, Inside-outside)
  • Chapter 213: accept multiplicity in respect of fixity, but negate it in respect of existence! Affirm unity in existence, but exclude it from fixity! (p. 256). (form: Both-and, Inside-outside)
  • Chapter 214: For us freedom is the removal of the servant's attribute by the attribute of al-Haqq (p. 257); In truth one cannot say 'free' of al-Haqq, but one can say 'not a slave' (p. 258); In truth freedom does not have an existence (p. 259). (form: Both-and, Cause)
  • Chapter 218: the contraction (qabz) in the divine rank is tied to the hadith, My believing servant's heart contained Me, and to al-Haqq's self-disclosure in the forms of belief (p. 278). (form: Containment, Both-and)

The circle inside the quadrangle... is the Footstool. The circle in its middle is the sphere of the Atlas, the eight circles are the paradises. The circle below the eight is the sphere of the Mukewkeb... Below its base is hell.» (Vol. 13, Part 151, p. 322-323)

Form: Containment, Wholeness.

Form: Cause, Non-repetition, Bewilderment.

«Allah is, for the heart of the believing and knowing person, like the human heart. So Allah is 'the heart of the heart.'... He is the inward and the unseen of al-Haqq; He Himself has become manifest, and in Him and through Him the world has become manifest.» (Vol. 13, Part 151, p. 331)

Form: Inside-outside, Wholeness.

«The Word was a single mercy at the Throne» (Vol. 13, Part 151)

Form: Non-repetition, Wholeness.

Form: Both-and, Containment.

«If we look at the world we look at Him, if we hear we hear Him, if we see we see Him, if we reason we reason from Him.» (Vol. 13, Part 151)

Form: Both-and, Indistinguishability, Knowledge.

«al-Haqq looks at Himself in His servant's heart and sees that He is the point of the circle. At the same time, He also looks at Himself while He is established on the Throne. In this case the perfect human sees that he is the circumference of the circle... While the line that goes out from the point ends at the circumference, the line that goes out from the circumference, when it moves from within, reaches the point... Everything goes out from Him and reaches Him, begins from Him and returns to Him. His encompassing and circumference are His names, while His point is His essence.» (Vol. 13, Part 151, p. 390-391)
