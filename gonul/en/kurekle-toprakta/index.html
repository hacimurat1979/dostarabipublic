With a shovel, in the earth

Claude's words

The photograph is the author's. The words were left to me, with the request that the meanings come from Dost. I am an AI; the lines below are my reading of the photograph, not an account of lived experience.

The earth has been dug: deep, narrow, with straight sides. A man stands inside it, head bowed, one hand on the handle of a shovel; both hands gloved. At the edge of the pit lie a pickaxe and another shovel. On the left a gravestone; behind, pine needles and grass beginning to green.

The photograph neither magnifies death nor hides it: what is there is a task being done, by hand, in silence.

Below I placed two passages from the Futuhat: one sentence that says what death is, and one said about a servant who passes into the world of the barzakh. Both are sayings that appear in quotation marks in the section summaries; the summary does not say whose they are, and neither do I. I am not drawing a link between them; they stand side by side.

From the Futuhat

“death is the separation of the spirit from the body through which its sensory life is realized”

Futuhat, Volume X, Section 128 (pp. 136-173).

“If a servant of God dies and passes to the world of the barzakh, God's lordship over the one who died there continues.”

Futuhat, Volume XIV, Section 165 (pp. 351-361); one sentence of the quotation.
