Principles of Wahdat al-Wujud

A reading from Dost's words

This page is not a reading summary. We look for the principles of what came to be called “wahdat al-wujud” after Dost in Dost's own accounts. The principle headings and the short reading sentences under them are our own working frame; the words in quotation marks are Dost's.

The quotations are taken from the site's part summaries (mostly Ekrem Demirli's translation for the Futuhat; A. Avni Konuk's translation and commentary for the Fusus) and have not been checked against the Arabic.

We do not say “the same” or “confirmed”; where a principle is not found in Dost in exactly this form, we say that too. The term itself is not Dost's own; it is a name given to the view later. The English and Portuguese texts are our rendering.

How to read this page

  1. Each principle opens with a short heading and sentence showing our reading frame.
  2. Below it stand Dost's words, with the source (volume, part, page) right under each.
  3. Then comes an example Dost himself gives, and last the place where Dost limits or distinguishes the principle.

Principle 1: Existence belongs to the Real.

What this work does not do

  • The quotations come not from Dost's Arabic text but from the site's Turkish part summaries; each was checked letter by letter against the summary, not against the Arabic.
  • A quoted sentence of the form “the Real is creation and creation is the Real”, the word “wujud”, and the triplet “from Him, to Him, in Him” do not occur in these summaries; the related principles are supported by Dost's own words such as “neither He nor other than He” and “we are He and we are not He”.
  • Principles 3 and 15 have weaker support: the language of levels comes mostly from Konuk's commentary; the triple formula for journeying does not occur in quotation.
  • For each principle Dost's own limitations are shown separately; among them are the places where he rejects incarnation (hulul) and union (ittihad). This page does not defend this view and does not arbitrate.
  • The examples are only those Dost himself gives; we added no analogy of our own.
  • The sequence of principles does not follow a fixed list: a table of contents of a book served as a guide for the main headings, but in Dost's way of speaking some headings occur not as separate principles but inside others.
