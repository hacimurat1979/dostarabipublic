Whoever Knows He Has Seen Has Not Seen — and the Gnostic Is Not Yet the End of the Road

pp. 136–144

Site reading summary

Chapter 442 says plainly: “Only he who knows he has not seen Him can know Him.” Chapter 441 sets up an order — the gnostic below the knower. Chapter 443 tells how the gnostic passes into the knower.

Read the part

Gnostic or Knower?

Chapter 441 says: “God's praising His chosen servants with knowledge is a more perfect praise than His praise of the gnostics.” The reason: “Knowledge is an attribute of God, whereas gnosis is not an attribute of His… The knower is the Real's mirror, while the gnostic or the jurist is not God's mirror.”

Yet the same chapter does not let the word “knowledge” off easily. It at once sets this measure: “one upon whom the fruit of his knowledge is not seen, or whose knowledge does not rule him, is not a knower but merely a transmitter.” And more plainly still: “when you see someone who claims knowledge yet does not accept the all-encompassing character of mercy, know that this person has no knowledge!”

Mercy Goes Ahead of Knowledge

Chapter 441 has this line: “mercy goes ahead of knowledge and seeks out the servant; then knowledge follows mercy.” He says this not as a wish but as a description of a road: “this is the road the folk of God and His elect walk upon.” And he gives its warrant: “We gave him knowledge from Us and taught him knowledge from Our presence.”

At the opening of 441 he shows one by one that where the verses speak of the gnostics they use words like “we have believed” and “our Lord,” and never say “we have known”: “Here He did not say ‘what they knew.'”

“Only He Who Knows He Has Not Seen Him Can Know Him”

The title of 442: “Whoever sees Me and knows that he has seen Me has not seen Me.” Its reason: to see is to encompass the seen in knowledge; yet “we know that the sight of one who sees the Real cannot hold Him.” Of what cannot be held one cannot say “I saw, and I know that I saw.” The sentence that follows: “So the one who sees the Real has in truth not seen Him. Hence only he who knows he has not seen Him can know Him.”

The Answer a Pronoun Brought

In 442 Ibn Arabi reads Moses' request through grammar: “The reason God said to him ‘you shall not see Me' was that Moses used the first person singular and said ‘let me look.'” And he adds: had he used the plural or another pronoun, “perhaps the answer would not have been ‘you shall not see Me.'” He does not offer this as a certainty — immediately after, he says “God knows best!”

Looking Four Times — and Falling into Bewilderment

Chapter 443 describes what someone sees when looking at a person created upon the divine form. The look is not one; it is repeated four times, and each time a different answer comes. Where it ends is not a piece of knowledge but a state: “you fall into bewilderment about him as you fall into bewilderment about God.”

Where Does the Effect Take Place?

In the middle of 443 there is a chain of questions left unanswered: “Is the effect of created things upon one another a sound state of affairs? Or is that effect the Real's effect in creatures? Or the creatures' effect in the Real? Or the Real's effect in the Real? Or is it all of these? Or is there in truth nothing that may be called ‘effect' at all?” Six possibilities are listed and none is chosen. Then the impasse is written out plainly: “Yet there is nothing but the Real and creation. This being so, where has the effect taken place?”

The chapter leaves this question unclosed and adds: “This matter is among the hard subjects the soul wishes to learn.” Just before this, having said “some people are vicegerent-humans and some are animal-humans,” he adds a further sentence: “And the seeker of this knowledge cannot know which of these classes he falls into.”
